\section{Household}

The representative household solves:

\begin{align}
\max_{\{C_t,I_t,K_{t+1}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}}
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \beta^t \ln C_t
\end{align}

subject to:

\begin{align}
C_t + I_t + T_t &= w_t L_t + r_t K_t + \Pi_t,
\end{align}

and the law of motion for capital:

\begin{align}
K_{t+1} &= (1-\delta)K_t + I_t.
\end{align}

The Euler equation is:

\begin{align}
\frac{1}{C_t}
=
\beta \frac{1}{C_{t+1}}
(1+r_{t+1}-\delta).
\end{align}

\section{Final Goods Sectors}

There are two final goods sectors, indexed by \(j \in \{1,2\}\). Sector \(j\) produces:

\begin{align}
Y_{j,t}
=
A_j K_{j,t}^{\alpha_j}
L_{j,t}^{\beta_j}
(\chi_t E_{j,t})^{1-\alpha_j-\beta_j}.
\end{align}

Aggregate output is:

\begin{align}
Y_t = Y_{1,t} + Y_{2,t}.
\end{align}

Sector \(j\) chooses capital, labor, and energy services to maximize profits:

\begin{align}
\Pi_{j,t}
=
Y_{j,t}
-
r_t K_{j,t}
-
w_t L_{j,t}
-
P_{E,j,t}E_{j,t}.
\end{align}

The first-order conditions are:

\begin{align}
r_t
&=
\alpha_j \frac{Y_{j,t}}{K_{j,t}},
\\
w_t
&=
\beta_j \frac{Y_{j,t}}{L_{j,t}},
\\
P_{E,j,t}
&=
(1-\alpha_j-\beta_j)
\frac{Y_{j,t}}{E_{j,t}}.
\end{align}

Under perfect competition and constant returns to scale:

\begin{align}
\Pi_{j,t}=0.
\end{align}

Equivalently:

\begin{align}
Y_{j,t}
=
r_t K_{j,t}
+
w_t L_{j,t}
+
P_{E,j,t}E_{j,t}.
\end{align}

Total final-goods-sector profits are:

\begin{align}
\Pi^Y_t
=
\sum_{j=1}^{2} \Pi_{j,t}.
\end{align}

\section{Energy Services}

Each final goods sector combines fossil and renewable energy into energy services:

\begin{align}
E_{j,t}
=
\left[
\omega_j E_{f,j,t}^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}}
+
(1-\omega_j)E_{r,j,t}^{\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}}
\right]^{\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}}.
\end{align}

The sector-specific energy price index is:

\begin{align}
P_{E,j,t}
=
\left[
\omega_j^{\sigma}P_{f,t}^{1-\sigma}
+
(1-\omega_j)^{\sigma}P_{r,t}^{1-\sigma}
\right]^{\frac{1}{1-\sigma}}.
\end{align}

Conditional demand for fossil energy is:

\begin{align}
E_{f,j,t}
=
\omega_j^{\sigma}
\left(
\frac{P_{f,t}}{P_{E,j,t}}
\right)^{-\sigma}
E_{j,t}.
\end{align}

Conditional demand for renewable energy is:

\begin{align}
E_{r,j,t}
=
(1-\omega_j)^{\sigma}
\left(
\frac{P_{r,t}}{P_{E,j,t}}
\right)^{-\sigma}
E_{j,t}.
\end{align}

\section{Energy Sector}

The energy sector produces fossil and renewable energy using final goods as inputs. Let \(M_{f,t}\) and \(M_{r,t}\) denote final goods used in fossil and renewable energy production.

Fossil energy production is:

\begin{align}
E_{f,t}
=
\frac{z_f}{c_f}M_{f,t}.
\end{align}

Renewable energy production is:

\begin{align}
E_{r,t}
=
\frac{z_r}{c_r}M_{r,t}.
\end{align}

Equivalently, the final-goods inputs required for energy production are:

\begin{align}
M_{f,t}
=
\frac{c_f}{z_f}E_{f,t},
\qquad
M_{r,t}
=
\frac{c_r}{z_r}E_{r,t}.
\end{align}

Under perfect competition, fossil and renewable energy prices are equal to their marginal private costs:

\begin{align}
P_{f,t}
&=
\frac{c_f}{z_f},
\\
P_{r,t}
&=
\frac{c_r}{z_r} - s_t.
\end{align}

The subsidy is assumed to satisfy:

\begin{align}
0 \leq s_t < \frac{c_r}{z_r}.
\end{align}

Energy-sector profits are:

\begin{align}
\Pi^E_t
=
P_{f,t}E_{f,t}
+
P_{r,t}E_{r,t}
-
M_{f,t}
-
M_{r,t}
+
s_tE_{r,t}.
\end{align}

Using the pricing equations and perfect competition:

\begin{align}
\Pi^E_t = 0.
\end{align}

Total profits rebated to households are:

\begin{align}
\Pi_t = \Pi^Y_t + \Pi^E_t.
\end{align}

\section{Government}

The government subsidizes renewable energy production. The subsidy is financed by a lump-sum tax on households:

\begin{align}
T_t = s_t E_{r,t}.
\end{align}

\section{Market Clearing}

Goods market clearing is:

\begin{align}
Y_t
=
C_t
+
I_t
+
M_{f,t}
+
M_{r,t}.
\end{align}

Using the energy production technologies:

\begin{align}
Y_t
=
C_t
+
I_t
+
\frac{c_f}{z_f}E_{f,t}
+
\frac{c_r}{z_r}E_{r,t}.
\end{align}

Capital market clearing is:

\begin{align}
K_t
=
K_{1,t}
+
K_{2,t}.
\end{align}

Labor market clearing is:

\begin{align}
L_t
=
L_{1,t}
+
L_{2,t}.
\end{align}

Fossil energy market clearing is:

\begin{align}
E_{f,t}
=
E_{f,1,t}
+
E_{f,2,t}.
\end{align}

Renewable energy market clearing is:

\begin{align}
E_{r,t}
=
E_{r,1,t}
+
E_{r,2,t}.
\end{align}

\section{Competitive Equilibrium}

A competitive equilibrium is a sequence of quantities:

\begin{align}
\{C_t,I_t,K_t,Y_t,Y_{1,t},Y_{2,t},E_{j,t},E_{f,j,t},E_{r,j,t},
E_{f,t},E_{r,t},M_{f,t},M_{r,t}\},
\qquad t=0,1,2,\ldots
\end{align}

and prices:

\begin{align}
\{w_t,r_t,P_{f,t},P_{r,t},P_{E,j,t}\},
\qquad t=0,1,2,\ldots
\end{align}

such that:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item the household satisfies its budget constraint, capital accumulation equation, and Euler equation;
    \item final goods firms satisfy their first-order conditions;
    \item final goods firms minimize the cost of energy services;
    \item the energy sector satisfies its production and pricing equations;
    \item the government budget constraint holds;
    \item goods, capital, labor, fossil energy, and renewable energy markets clear.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Steady State}

In steady state, all variables are constant over time. The Euler equation implies:

\begin{align}
r
=
\frac{1}{\beta}
-
1
+
\delta.
\end{align}

Investment satisfies:

\begin{align}
I = \delta K.
\end{align}

The steady-state resource constraint is:

\begin{align}
Y
=
C
+
\delta K
+
\frac{c_f}{z_f}E_f
+
\frac{c_r}{z_r}E_r.
\end{align}
